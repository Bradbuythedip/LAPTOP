This page is for whoever sets the launch parameters, not for buyers. It does arithmetic on numbers you type. It does not ask you to sign anything, cannot send a transaction, and does not deploy or configure anything — it tells you what a fee design would do so you can pick one before it is set in a contract.

The pool at launch

“Buys at zero tax” is your estimate of launch-day demand if you charged nothing, and it changes the answer more than it looks like it should. Once total spending is large next to the seed, the pool fee and the sell tax already capture that volume, and a buy tax mostly deters the buyers who would have generated it — so the best buy tax falls as you expect more demand, and can reach zero. What a buyer pays

Publish this

The checker flags other tokens for undisclosed taxes. If LAPTOP has one and this site does not say so, the checker fails its own test. Put this on the launch page and in the token's docs, and a buyer who reads it cannot say they were surprised.

What this does not tell you. It has no opinion on whether a tax is a good idea, and it cannot tell you what demand will actually be — both the buyer count and the sensitivity are your estimates, not measurements. It models one buy size repeated; real flow is a mix of sizes, and large buys pay disproportionately more to price impact. It assumes every buyer sells exactly once, which overstates sell-tax income if some hold and understates it if some trade repeatedly.

What a tax cannot do. It cannot make a launch succeed. Every point of tax is a point of worse execution for the person buying, and it shows up in the size curve and in the venue comparison like any other cost. A design that pays you most is not the same as a design people want to buy into, and this page only answers the first.
