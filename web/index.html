How it works

Three mechanisms and one list of things they do not do. The fourth panel is the one worth reading twice.

1One order, at one price

Everyone who gets in before launch is pooled into a single buy. One transaction, one price, split by what each of you put in. Nobody in the pool can front-run anybody else in the pool, because there is only one order to be in front of.

2You sell at yesterday's price

If the price is above its 24-hour average and you sell today, the difference burns. At or below the average, you sell at today's price and nothing burns. The haircut is destroyed rather than collected — unless the launcher took a share of it, which is capped at a fifth of the haircut and fixed at deployment. supplyOnlyFalls() on the token answers that in one word, and until a token exists there is nothing to ask.

3Nobody can nuke it

No wallet moves more than 20% of its balance per day, whales included. It applies to every transfer out, so splitting across fresh wallets is throttled at the same rate — the first hop is itself a transfer. One address is exempt, and the panel beside this one says which and why.

!What the rules do not do

  • One wallet is outside both rules, permanently. Seeding a pool and selling into it are the same transfer, so the launcher has to be exempt from the cap to seed at all — and the flag that skips the cap skips the burn as well. freeze() makes it permanent. It is the wallet holding the whole supply the moment the token exists. Read capExempt() on it yourself, and look at where the supply went afterwards.
  • Before freeze(), none of it is settled. While frozen() is false the admin can exempt any address, its own included. Launching through the launchpad freezes in the same transaction, so there is no window — but that is a property of how it was launched, not of the token. Read frozen() before you believe any of this panel.
  • The dial is also your instant loss. A burn of 41% means selling burns 41% — including if you bought ten minutes ago at the top.
  • A slow bleed is free. Rule 1 only bites above the 24-hour average. Sell into a falling market and nothing burns at all.
  • A cap is not a lock. 20% a day compounds: a wallet is 67% out in five days and basically empty in a fortnight.
  • An unregistered venue is outside both rules. Liquidity that migrates to a wrapper the token has never heard of takes the rules off.
  • No oracle, no Rule 1. If the price feed is not ready the burn is zero and the token is an ordinary ERC-20 with a transfer cap.
  • Nothing here has been audited, and nothing has been on a testnet.

Getting in before launch

The pooled buy takes deposits, buys once at launch, and hands out tokens pro-rata. Until it exists there is nothing to send anything to.

Do not just send ETH to it. The contract refuses plain transfers from anyone except the router — your send will revert and you will pay gas for nothing. Getting in means calling deposit() with ETH attached, from your own wallet.
This page cannot do it for you, and that is deliberate. It connects and reads, and it does not ask you to sign anything. You can leave before the buy for a fee, and that fee stays in the pool for the people who did not leave — it is never paid to the deployer. If the buy never happens, refunds open at the date above: no permission and no fee, but you have to call refund() yourself.
