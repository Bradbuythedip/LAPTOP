What connects them

One thing, and it is a : whatever $SNOOZE you hold at one block, fixed in advance, is your place in LAPTOP. You claim it yourself, from your own wallet. Nothing is locked and nothing is taken.

Buying $SNOOZE the hour before and selling the hour after is the worst way to use it: everyone doing that at once pushes the price above its own average, which is exactly when selling burns most. It pays holders, not renters.

What this does not do

  • Virtual liquidity is not liquidity. Before bonding, the only ETH behind the price is what buyers put in. The curve cannot pay out more than that, and it does not pretend to.
  • The burn is also your instant loss. A 41% dial means selling burns 41%, including ten minutes after you bought.
  • A falling market burns nothing. The rule only bites above the average.
  • One wallet is outside both rules — the launcher's, which has to be exempt to seed at all. the detail
  • Slippage gets 46% worse at bonding, not better. Only the real ETH goes into the pool; the virtual part does not exist to move. The curve is the deepest the book ever is.
  • It is not permissionless yet. One wallet can deploy, and that wallet is the launcher's. Anyone can BUY; nobody else can launch until a different contract ships.
  • Most tokens never bond. That is the normal outcome, not the failure case.
  • No audit, no testnet run. Nothing here has been either.
